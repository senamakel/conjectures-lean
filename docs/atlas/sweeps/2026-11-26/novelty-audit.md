statement**, not the Lean proof, is already public. Searches ran on 2026-11-26 over arXiv (exa),
erdosproblems.com pages and discussion threads, the erdosproblems.ai wiki (a mirror with claim

### Notes per item

**a.** The [Er79d] passage reads: "It seems certain that for every k there are infinitely many
values of n for which max_{n−k<m<n}(m+d(m)) ≤ n+2, though this is hopeless with our present
methods. It would easily follow from hypothesis H of Schinzel." Open formal-conjectures PR #4407
("sharpen Erdős #647 candidate reductions") links the first open window depth to the infinitude
of Sophie Germain primes, but proves no implication from H. A referee would call our proof the
routine CRT-plus-Dickson argument Erdős had in mind. It is new as a written proof, but it is
folklore.

**b.** We did not find these three bounds stated anywhere. One finding needs a check against the
journal version. Bazzanella's Theorem 2 states `O(f(N) log² N)` exceptions with
`[n², (n+1)²] ⊂ [1, N]`, but his proof bounds the dyadic set `A_δ(N) = {√N ≤ n ≤ √(2N)}` only.
Summing that bound over dyadic blocks gives `O(f(N) log³ N)` on `[1, N]`. Read on `[1, N]`, the
published argument therefore gives what ours gives, up to `f`. For "at least one prime", Selberg's
large-gap bound already gives `O(log² X)` per dyadic block, with no `f`: every exception lies in
a gap of length `≥ 2√X`, so the count is at most `Σ d/(2√X) ≪ log² X`. Bazzanella's "expected
number of primes" is a stronger property, so the two results cannot be compared directly. Our
README's sentence "Bazzanella … one logarithm better" should be re-read with this in mind.

**c.** The constant 2 is public. The sharper question is still open, and Bach raised it himself
(1990, end of §6): his computations "showed that in Theorem 2, one can replace '2' by
2/log 3 = 1.657…, which is the best possible constant. Possibly this is also true of Theorem 3,
but no attempt has been made to verify such a claim." Theorem 3 is the prime-to-`m` form, which
is the one generators need. A brute-force computation here confirms that `m = 3` forces 1.6571:
over `3 ≤ m < 6000` the worst ratios are 1.657 (`m = 3`), 1.561 (`m = 4`), 1.557 (`m = 6`), 1.287
(`m = 24`), and the ratio stays below 1.06 for every `m ≥ 25`.

**d.** The asymptotic statement (iii) under GRH now has a written proof (MacKenzie). The explicit
all-`n` bound is still ours, as far as we found. The constant has a lot of room. A direct
computation of `h(n)` for `n ≤ 3000` finds `h(n) > P(n)` at 1,529 values of `n`. The largest
ratio `h(n)/log² n` is 2.49 (`n = 3`), then 1.16 (`n = 5`), and it stays below 0.87 for every
`n ≥ 11`.

**e.** Two points beyond the verdict. (1) IEANTN (node DudekPlatt.v3) records a defect in
Dudek–Platt's Lemma 2.1, the unconditional pipeline, which affects Axler's `e^{3158.442}`. Our
proof goes through their RH Lemma 3.2 and Schoenfeld instead, so the defect does not touch it.
(2) Computed thresholds (worst case, `π = li ± E`). Schoenfeld alone on both sides fails up to
`log x = 39.47` (`x ≈ 1.39·10¹⁷`), so our `e⁴⁰` is within 0.53 of what this method can give.
Adding `π(x) < li(x)` on the left lowers the threshold to `log x = 36.98` (`1.15·10¹⁶`, exactly
Dudek–Platt's value). Büthe's 2018 two-sided bound reaches `log x = 30.41`.

**f.** Computed with the CMS bound `22/25` (cramer-rh, tier U). With no finite check at all, it
gives every real `α ≥ 2.629`. With a finite check, the threshold `X₀(α)` below which the check is
needed, and the number of chain primes `≈ X₀^{1/α}` (consecutive α-th roots less than 1 apart),
are:

| `α` | `log₁₀ X₀` | chain primes | Oppermann check needed to `N = √X₀` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.5 | 8.3 | 2·10³ | 10^4.1 |
| 2.4 | 12.1 | 1.1·10⁵ | 10^6.1 |
| 2.35 | 14.9 | 2.2·10⁶ | 10^7.4 |
| 2.3 | 18.6 | 1.3·10⁸ | 10^9.3 |
| 2.25 | 24.0 | 4.8·10¹⁰ | 10^12.0 |
| 2.2253 | 27.7 | — | 10^13.8 (SW's `7.05·10¹³`) |
| 2.21 | 30.4 | — | 10^15.2 |
| 2.20 | 32.5 | — | 10^16.2 |
| 2.18 | 37.3 | — | 10^18.7 |

So `α ≥ 2.22524` is the exact limit of CMS combined with SW's computation, which reproduces CS's
0.2253. Getting below 9/4 needs an Oppermann verification beyond `7.05·10¹³` (`10¹⁵` for
`α = 2.212`, `10¹⁶` for `2.202`). That is a non-Lean computation beyond the state of the art, and
certifying it in Lean is out of reach. The one technical difference from CS: our interval is open
on the left, while theirs is `[x^α, (x+1)^α − 1)`. A referee would call that trivial.

**g.** Our implications row is right to attribute these to Ferreira–Mariano. The tier-H input
`Firoozbakht.firoozbakht_conjecture_consequence` (fc, `research solved`, body `sorry`) is their
Theorem 2.2. Its proof needs Dusart's `π(x) > x/(ln x − 1 − 1/ln x − 1/ln² x)` for
`x ≥ 1,772,201` and a finite check up to `n = 133,114`.

## The three most promising targets

1. **Bach's sharp generator constant `2/log² 3` for every `m ≥ 3`** (Theorem 3 form). Bach left it
   open in 1990 in so many words, and we found no later claim. Bellotti 2026 still quotes 2.
   Inputs we already have: LLS Thm 1.1 and the Bach-2 checker in lls-grh, and `generators_of_bach`
   in bach-corollaries-grh. Risk: the explicit-formula bound may not reach 1.657 between `10⁶`
   and `10³⁵`. Size that in Python with the lls-grh checker's inequality before any Lean work.
   Effort M to L: about 1 day to size, then 1–3 weeks; the checker re-run costs CPU-hours.
2. **Erdős 770(iii) with a single-digit explicit constant for all `n ≥ 2`**, for example
   `h(n) ≤ max(P(n), ⌈5 log² n⌉)`. It is not in any source found, and it is a strict
   strengthening of our own `C = 200`. Effort M: the two-case argument exists, and the work is in
   sharpening the product-counting case and running a finite check for small `n`, which costs
   CPU-minutes.
3. **Explicit per-block exceptional-set bounds for Legendre, Oppermann, Andrica and Brocard**,
   `≤ C log² X` exceptions with `n² ∈ [X, 2X]`, with explicit `C` and `X₀`. Combined with the
   published verifications, this gives "at most `C log³ N` exceptions, none below `7·10¹³`". This
   is new but routine, and it also settles the Bazzanella `[1, N]` reading noted under b.
   Effort S to M: the work is an explicit Selberg constant, which an explicit Cramér/Schoenfeld
   mean-value bound already in schoenfeld-rh or cramer-rh may supply.

Second priority (published, but it would discharge a formal-conjectures `sorry`): prove
`Firoozbakht.firoozbakht_conjecture_consequence` (Ferreira–Mariano Thm 2.2) in Lean. Effort L:
Dusart's bound is not in Lean at our pin, and PrimeNumberTheoremAnd's Dusart module carries
`sorry`s, according to IEANTN.
