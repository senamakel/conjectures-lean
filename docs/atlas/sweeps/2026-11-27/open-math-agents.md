## Split graphs of connectivity at most two

Let $G$ be a connected split graph with at least two vertices. Write its partition as a clique $C$ and an independent set $I$, put $A_c=N(c)\cap I$ for $c\in C$, and let $k=\kappa(G)$. If the largest induced tree has at most $k+2$ vertices and $k\le2$, then $G$ has a Hamiltonian path. This is a restricted case of Written on the Wall II #199. The Formal Conjectures statement landed in #3796. Searches by target name and by “traceable split graph” surfaced no proof PR in either catalogue on the search date. [Problem statement](https://lefttosolve.com/p/WrittenOnTheWallII.GraphConjecture199.conjecture199) · [Statement PR](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/3796)

A star centred at $c$ gives $|A_c|$ ≤ $k+1$. The edge $cd$, with the vertices in the symmetric difference of $A_c$ and $A_d$ attached as leaves, gives $|A_c\triangle A_d|$ ≤ $k$. Each member of $I$ has at least $k$ neighbours in $C$. If $I$ is empty, order the clique arbitrarily.

- **$k=1$.** If every row $A_c$ has size at most one, any two nonempty rows agree. Thus there is one independent vertex, which can start the path. If $A_c=\{a,b\}$, then every other row is a nonempty subset of it. With a second clique vertex $d$, name a common neighbour $b\in A_d\cap A_c$ and start $a,c,b,d$, then append the rest of the clique. With a single clique vertex use $a,c,b$.
- **$k=2$, rows of size at most two.** Choose two incident clique edges per independent vertex. The resulting bipartite graph consists of paths with clique endpoints, isolated clique vertices, and cycles. Clique vertices on a cycle have both original independent neighbours in that cycle. Two cycles would therefore give disjoint size-two rows, contradicting the symmetric-difference bound. Open the sole cycle, if present, into a path with one independent endpoint. Concatenate the components through clique endpoints, starting with that path.
- **$k=2$, a row $A_c=A$ of size three.** All rows meet $A$. Each row with an outsider is a pair from $A$ together with exactly one outsider. If there are two or more outsiders, rows of distinct outsiders must use the same pair $\{p,q\}\subset A$. Their clique neighbourhoods are disjoint and each has size at least two. For the third member $r\in A$, choose $d\ne c$ adjacent to $r$. Start $q,c,r,d$, join a two-edge path through each outsider between two of its clique neighbours, and finish at $p$. Insert any unused clique vertices into a clique-to-clique link.
- **Exactly one outsider $z$.** Choose distinct $x,y\in N(z)$. If there is an extra clique vertex $d\notin\{c,x,y\}$, take $a\in A_d\cap A$ and $b\in(A_x\cap A)\setminus\{a\}$, with $e$ the third element of $A$. Use $e,c,a,d$, all unused clique vertices, then $y,z,x,b$. If $C=\{c,x,y\}$, the labels missed by $x,y$ differ by minimum degree two. Write them as $a,b$, respectively, and use $b,x,z,y,a,c,e$.
- **Zero outsiders.** If the clique has two vertices, both see all of $A$ by minimum degree two, so alternate. Otherwise, take distinct $d,e\in C\setminus\{c\}$ with distinct neighbours $a\in A_d$, $b\in A_e$. These exist because every row meets $A$ and the union of rows other than $A_c$ is $A$. With $r$ the remaining member use $a,d$, the unused clique vertices, then $e,b,c,r$.

The same star and double-star argument gives the exact split-graph induced-tree formula: take the larger of one plus the largest row size and two plus the largest symmetric-difference size over distinct clique vertices. Every induced tree uses at most two clique vertices. With two, its independent vertices must see exactly one of the two. The formula omits the second term for a one-vertex clique.

## Largest denominator in a family of Erdős–Straus decompositions

Let $t\ge1$ and suppose $p=4t^2+2t-1$ is prime. Among representations of $4/p$ as three unit fractions with distinct positive denominators, the smallest possible largest denominator is $p(t+1)$, attained uniquely up to order by $t(t+1),pt,p(t+1)$.

The displayed solution follows by clearing denominators. In any solution at least one denominator is divisible by $p$, because otherwise clearing denominators and reducing modulo $p$ gives a contradiction. Three multiples are impossible since their reciprocal sum is at most $3/p$. In the one-multiple case write it $pw$. Reducing the cleared identity after cancelling $p$ gives $4w\equiv1\pmod p$. For $t\ge2$, $w\ge(p+1)/4>t+1$. When $t=1$, $p=5$ and $w\equiv4\pmod 5$.

In the two-multiple case write the denominators as $x,pu,pv$ with $u<v$ and $p\nmid x$. Then $x(4uv-u-v)=puv$, so the positive quantity $4uv-u-v$ is divisible by $p$. Since $u\le v-1$, it is at most $4v^2-6v+1$. The last expression increases for integer $v\ge2$, and equals $p$ at $v=t+1$. Thus $v\ge t+1$, with equality forcing $u=t$ and $x=t(t+1)$. The existence identity is a special case of the previously published Type II parameterization. The optimality question is a refinement and leaves the general Erdős–Straus conjecture open. [Erdős #242](https://www.erdosproblems.com/242) · [Type II parametrization](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16817)

Lean 4.36.0 verifies existence, the lower bound on the largest denominator, and the uniqueness of the minimizing increasing triple in `Implications.ErdosStrausOptimal.family_optimal`. The theorem `isRep_iff_unitFractions` identifies the denominator-cleared equation with the equality over the rationals when all denominators are positive. Both declarations print `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`. [Lean source](../../../../conjectures/implications/Implications/ErdosStrausOptimal.lean)

## A length-eight consecutive-pattern equivalence

The patterns 16237485 and 16247385 are super-strongly c-Wilf equivalent. Fix any permutation length and any set of marked starts. On permutations containing the first pattern at every marked start, swap the fourth and sixth entries of every marked window. Two overlapping windows can start only five or seven positions apart, so the swaps are disjoint. The only effect of a swap on a second window is that a start five positions later receives a new first entry. Its new value has rank at most four in the earlier window and is still below the later window's second-smallest entry, which has rank five in the earlier window. Thus each marked window becomes the second pattern. The same argument in reverse gives an involutive bijection. Inclusion–exclusion over sets containing the marked starts gives equality for permutations with exactly the prescribed start set.

Lean 4.36.0 checked the finite facts that both patterns have overlap shifts exactly 5 and 7 and that swapping positions four and six interchanges the patterns. Each check used kernel `decide`, with `propext` as its only printed axiom. [Lean check](WilfOverlap.lean) The arbitrary-length bijection and equality of all exact occurrence counts now compile in Lean. [Full proof](../../../../conjectures/implications/Implications/WilfPatternSwap.lean) The exact pair is among the unresolved classes in the published length-eight audit, and targeted GitHub PR searches surfaced no matching proof. [Chuang's paper and reproducibility archive](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24052489)

### Bounded sorting-network search

The verified Dobbelaere 51-comparator seed is listed in [proof-foundry PR #41](https://github.com/steph4n-gh/proof-foundry/pull/41). Checked the seed and each candidate against the weight-sorted output on all 16,384 Boolean inputs, packed into one bitset per wire. At each contiguous window position, kept the original prefix and suffix and tried every ordered sequence of replacement comparators on the 14 wires, with repetitions allowed. The search exhausted all 4,550 two-to-one replacements (0.071 CPU seconds) and all 405,769 three-to-two replacements (3.901 CPU seconds, under the 30-second cap). Neither search found a sorting network. These runs cover only those replacement families for this seed. Tomar's lower bound leaves the optimum at 50 or 51. [Lower-bound proof](https://zenodo.org/records/24299513)

A second pass exhausted the four-to-three and five-to-four windows of the same seed. A fixed 64-input bit-packed sample (MT19937 seed 20261127) rejected most candidates. The full 16,384-input verifier rejected all 314,358 and 11,667,307 sample survivors, respectively. The four-to-three run checked 36,171,408 candidates in 0.72 CPU seconds, and the five-to-four run checked 3,223,023,167 in 61.62 CPU seconds. The exact 120-second wall cap was reached by neither run. The result covers these contiguous replacements for this comparator order, including repeats in the replacement, with all comparator directions oriented from the lower to the higher wire.

The 42-vertex extension search exhausted the three published 41-vertex seeds. The compatibility graph joins vertices having no common neighbour in the seed. Each search stopped after finding every clique of size at most six, with no six-clique found. Counts by clique size zero through six were [1, 41, 94, 45, 16, 3, 0] for the first two seeds and [1, 41, 94, 47, 16, 3, 0] for the third. Parsing and enumeration took 0.0009 CPU seconds. The seed file SHA-256 is 900da21cbbb59d10c30f26feb35ebab4f0e3ef65f21429939584bda497768989. The catalogue does not establish completeness of the seed list. [Seed file](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/data/extremal/c4_n41e132.maybe.s6)

## Lean follow-up

- Erdős #932 now has [a finite cutoff theorem](../../../../conjectures/implications/Implications/SmoothGapCounting.lean) combining telescoped gap lengths with disjoint smooth interiors. It bounds the count up to prime index N. The smooth-number estimate is a separate input to the asymptotic rate.
- The [42-vertex deletion reduction](../../../../conjectures/implications/Implications/C4Extremal42.lean) connects the counts to actual induced subgraphs. It proves the 138-edge upper bound given the 132-edge bound on all 41-vertex deletions, and gives a degree-six deletion attaining 132 when the original has 138 edges. The degree-six neighborhood has no shared neighbor inside the deletion, so proving that no extremal 41-vertex deletion has a compatible six-set gives an upper bound of 137. The [three seed certificates](../../../../conjectures/implications/Implications/C4SeedCertificates.lean) now use kernel computation. Applying them to every deletion still needs a complete classification of the extremal seeds.
- The [Wilf counting reduction](../../../../conjectures/implications/Implications/WilfEquivalence.lean) turns bijections for all prescribed sets of starts into equality of counts for each exact occurrence set. The [full proof](../../../../conjectures/implications/Implications/WilfPatternSwap.lean) verifies that the simultaneous swap changes every marked occurrence to the other pattern. The exact count theorem prints `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`.
- The split-graph development verifies the [induced star](../../../../conjectures/implications/Implications/SplitGraphTrees.lean) and the [two-vertex bound](../../../../conjectures/implications/Implications/SplitGraphPath.lean) on the independent side at connectivity one. The double-star construction and Hamiltonian path remain to be formalized.
